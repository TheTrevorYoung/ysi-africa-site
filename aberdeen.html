YSI Africa is developing an evidence-led Aberdeen Creek protection, baseline and ecological recovery programme. The current phase is designed to establish the site evidence, authority pathway, community context and technical basis needed to decide what future intervention — if any — is appropriate.

Protected wetland context

Aberdeen Creek sits within an internationally important estuary.

Aberdeen Creek is within the Sierra Leone River Estuary Ramsar Site (No. 1014). The original Ramsar site map identifies Aberdeen Creek as a core zone within the wider designated site.

That context makes evidence, authority coordination and ecological diagnosis essential before physical action. YSI is not presenting itself as the wetland manager or as having authority to undertake restoration independently.

Current work

From advocacy to a defensible environmental operating model.

The project is being built around evidence, permissions, ecological diagnosis, community context and clear decision gates.

Authority & permissions

YSI is continuing Aberdeen coordination with the National Protected Area Authority and is seeking current boundary, management, access and activity-specific guidance.

Field baseline & GIS

The evidence package is designed around repeatable ground photographs, GPS points, lawful observation, historical comparison and authoritative boundary information.

Waste-leakage pathways

YSI is examining how urban waste and pollution may move toward the creek so source-control responses can be designed outside sensitive habitat where appropriate.

Ecological diagnosis

The project will distinguish intact areas, degraded areas, natural regeneration, hydrological constraints and other stressors before proposing restoration actions.

Community & resource use

Local knowledge, legitimate resource use, access, livelihoods and stewardship are part of the baseline rather than an afterthought.

Duplication control

YSI is mapping existing and historical restoration activity so any future role is complementary rather than parallel or duplicative.

Technical doctrine

Restoration is not the same thing as planting trees.

YSI's working restoration doctrine follows ecological mangrove-restoration principles: understand why degradation occurred, reduce or remove relevant stressors, protect or restore the conditions that allow recovery, prioritize natural regeneration, monitor results, and use targeted planting only where site-specific evidence shows it is necessary.

This reduces the risk of planting the wrong species, in the wrong place, under conditions that cannot support long-term recovery.

Global Mangrove Alliance best-practice guidance →

Technical status: this is the project's operating doctrine, not a claim that a hydrological, ecological or biodiversity assessment has already been completed at Aberdeen Creek.

Institutional engagement

Relationships are described precisely.

National Protected Area Authority (NPAA): YSI has current Aberdeen Creek communication. This establishes a contact and coordination pathway; it is not presented as project approval or partnership.

Mangrove Action Project: YSI is in an active technical exchange. MAP has requested site context, GPS points, ground-level photographs and project information before deeper technical discussion. This is a technical contact relationship, not a formal partnership.

Environment Protection Agency Sierra Leone (EPA-SL): YSI has an existing institutional contact pathway. EPA directed Aberdeen-specific coordination toward NPAA; future EPA engagement remains relevant where a defined activity raises EPA-specific regulatory questions.

Relationship rule: an institution is not described as a partner, funder, endorser or approving authority unless that status is documented.

Evidence standard

The project will be built so its claims can be defended.

YSI's baseline protocol separates observation from inference, preserves dates and provenance, records repeatable observation points, and treats stakeholder statements as attributed input rather than automatically verified fact.

Quantitative Aberdeen-specific claims will not be published as verified results unless the underlying source, method, geography, period and unit can be defended.

The immediate evidence priorities are authoritative boundary/GIS information, current georeferenced photographs, site-access and safety review, current NPAA guidance, mapping of previous restoration footprints, and a clearer picture of waste and pollution pathways.
