Aberdeen Creek · Freetown · Development stage

YSI Africa is developing an evidence-led Aberdeen Creek protection, baseline and ecological recovery programme. The current phase is focused on understanding the site, coordinating with competent authorities, identifying waste and other pressures, and preparing technically defensible restoration decisions before any physical intervention.

Protected wetland context

Aberdeen Creek sits within an internationally important estuary.

Aberdeen Creek is within the Sierra Leone River Estuary Ramsar Site (No. 1014). The original Ramsar site map identifies Aberdeen Creek as a core zone within the wider designated site.

That status strengthens the case for a careful, authority-aligned approach. YSI is not presenting itself as the wetland manager or as having authority to undertake restoration independently.

Current work

From advocacy to a defensible environmental operating model.

The project is being built around evidence, permissions, ecological diagnosis and clear decision gates.

Authority & permissions

YSI is continuing institutional communication with the National Protected Area Authority (NPAA) and is seeking the current information needed to understand boundaries, management intentions, access and activity-specific requirements.

Field baseline & GIS

The first evidence package is designed around repeatable ground photographs, GPS points, lawful observation, historical comparison and authoritative boundary information.

Waste-leakage pathways

YSI is examining how urban waste and pollution may move toward the creek so future source-control measures can be designed outside sensitive habitat where appropriate.

Ecological diagnosis

The project will distinguish intact areas, degraded areas, natural regeneration, possible hydrological constraints and other stressors before proposing restoration actions.

Community & resource use

Local knowledge, legitimate resource use, access, livelihoods and stewardship are part of the baseline rather than an afterthought to restoration design.

Duplication control

YSI is mapping existing and historical restoration activity so any future role is complementary rather than a parallel or duplicative intervention.

Technical doctrine

Restoration is not the same thing as planting trees.

YSI's working restoration doctrine follows ecological mangrove-restoration principles: understand why degradation occurred, reduce or remove the relevant stressors, protect or restore the conditions that allow recovery, prioritize natural regeneration, monitor results, and use targeted planting only where site-specific evidence shows it is necessary.

This approach is intended to reduce the risk of planting the wrong species, in the wrong place, under conditions that cannot support long-term recovery.

Technical status: This is the project's operating doctrine, not a claim that YSI has already completed a hydrological, ecological or biodiversity assessment at Aberdeen Creek.

Institutional & technical engagement

Relationships are described precisely.

NPAA: YSI has current communication with the National Protected Area Authority concerning Aberdeen Creek. This is an institutional contact pathway; it is not presented as project approval or partnership.

Mangrove Action Project: YSI is in an active technical exchange. MAP has requested site context, GPS points, ground-level photographs and project information before deeper technical discussion. This is a technical contact relationship, not a claim of formal partnership.

EPA Sierra Leone: YSI has an existing institutional contact pathway with EPA-SL. For Aberdeen Creek-specific coordination, YSI has been directed toward NPAA; future EPA engagement remains relevant where defined activities raise EPA-specific regulatory questions.

YSI relationship rule: an institution is never described as a partner, funder, endorser or approving authority unless that status is actually documented.

What has not happened

Development stage means development stage.

No planting claim

YSI has not launched an Aberdeen mangrove planting campaign under this project.

No restoration-area claim

No hectares restored, survival rate or biodiversity-recovery statistic is being claimed by YSI.

No physical engineering claim

No YSI channel opening, hydrological alteration, interception structure or habitat modification is represented as completed or authorized.

No funding claim

The project is not presented as funded unless and until a funding commitment is documented.

No authority claim

YSI does not claim ownership, management authority or regulatory jurisdiction over Aberdeen Creek.

No invented partnership

Technical contacts, authorities and prospective collaborators retain their actual documented status.

Evidence standard

The project will be built so its claims can be defended.

YSI's baseline protocol separates observation from inference, preserves dates and provenance, records repeatable observation points, and treats stakeholder statements as attributed input rather than automatically verified fact.

Quantitative Aberdeen-specific claims will not be published as YSI-verified figures unless the underlying source, method, geography, period and unit can be defended.

The immediate evidence priorities are authoritative boundary/GIS information, current georeferenced photographs, site-access and safety review, current NPAA guidance, mapping of previous restoration footprints, and a clearer picture of waste and pollution pathways.

Technical contribution
