Projects & evidence

Work already undertaken. Systems still being built.

We separate completed or documented work from ideas still in development. That distinction matters.

Documented field work

Lumley Beach Cleanup

Cleanup and sanitation work at Lumley Beach helped establish the foundation for YSI's environmental mission. The goal is to move from episodic cleanup toward durable maintenance and waste-management systems.

Environmental advocacy

Aberdeen Creek Protection

YSI has raised concerns with relevant institutions about the protection of Aberdeen Creek and the wider environmental importance of Sierra Leone's coastal wetlands.

Development stage

Waste-to-Value & Resource Recovery

YSI is developing practical concepts around recycling, material recovery and responsible waste-to-value pathways. These are presented as development-stage concepts until evidence and partnerships support expansion.

About YSI

Independent, community-driven and nonpartisan.

Young Sustainability Initiative was created to give environmental work in Sierra Leone a focused institutional home. Its work is community-driven and designed to engage people across generations.

The organization's focus is straightforward: identify practical environmental problems, build credible solutions, document the work and develop partnerships capable of sustaining impact over time.

Transparency & accountability

Credibility comes before scale.

YSI will not claim staff, partners, projects or impact numbers that cannot be supported. Public claims are intentionally conservative while the evidence library, governance records and project documentation are strengthened.

01Evidence before claimsImpact numbers stay limited to what can be documented.
02Real partnerships onlyOrganizations are never presented as partners without a genuine relationship.
03Clear project statusField work, advocacy and development-stage concepts are labeled separately.
04Community-centered executionProjects are built for local relevance, durability and responsible stewardship.
