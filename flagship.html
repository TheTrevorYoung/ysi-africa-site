Freetown Circular Waste Recovery Project · Current stage: Phase 0

Measure first.
Build second.

YSI Africa is developing the Freetown Circular Waste Recovery Project as a localized waste-leakage and resource-recovery initiative. The current proposition is Phase 0 — Evidence & Operational Design: identify where leakage persists, measure what reaches the environment, characterize it, understand legitimate recovery and routing options, determine real costs and decide what additional infrastructure — if any — is justified.

From cleanup to system change

Cleanup exposed the problem. Measurement is the next step.

YSI's Lumley Beach field work provides direct experience with mixed coastal waste, cleanup logistics, collection and material-handling constraints.

That experience showed why removing waste after it reaches the environment is not enough. Phase 0 asks where leakage occurs, what reaches marine and wetland environments, what can realistically be intercepted or recovered, where material should go, what it costs and what infrastructure is genuinely missing.

Phase 0 is non-thermal. It excludes RDF/SRF preparation, combustion, waste-to-energy, thermal treatment and ash handling.

Its job is to produce the evidence needed for a later GO / NO-GO / MODIFY decision.

What Phase 0 must establish

Evidence a funder, technical reviewer and public authority can use.

01Leakage geographyWhere identifiable waste leakage occurs and which sites are suitable for lawful measurement or testing.
02Quantity & compositionWhat reaches the environment, how much, what it contains and how conditions vary over time.
03Pathways & sourcesProbable movement pathways and source hypotheses only to the level the methodology can defend.
04Recovery & routingWhat can realistically be recovered and which legitimate existing systems can receive each material stream.
05Real operating costInterception, handling, transport, measurement and routing costs — including cost per kg where defensible.

Public system logic

Intercept → Measure → Sort → Recover → Route → Learn → Replicate.

The main public story stays focused on the operating questions YSI can responsibly test now.

01InterceptTest lawful ways to capture identifiable leakage before it reaches sensitive environments.
02MeasureWeigh, record and link material to location, time and method.
03SortCharacterize the material stream and identify hazards, recoverables and residuals.
04RecoverAssess realistic reuse, recycling or other recovery pathways supported by evidence.
05RouteUse legitimate existing infrastructure and approved providers wherever practical.
06LearnMeasure costs, bottlenecks, environmental controls and what the system is actually missing.
07ReplicateOnly after a proven model exists and evidence supports expansion.

Possible technical recovery pathways

RDF/SRF remains a hypothesis — not the current project.

Some residual material may eventually warrant assessment for RDF/SRF or another technical recovery route. YSI is not assuming that outcome. Any later pathway would require supporting waste characterization, moisture and calorific-value evidence, engineering review, economics, environmental safeguards, emissions controls, regulation and a legitimate offtake/use case.

Phase 0 does not include thermal processing. Fundraising for a resource-recovery unit or full capital system remains a later decision, not the present proposition.

Decision rule: technical options follow the waste evidence. Grant availability does not dictate engineering.

What Phase 0 produces

A decision, not a predetermined facility.

  • defensible measurement evidence;
  • recovery and routing analysis;
  • real operating-cost evidence;
  • permission and provider requirements;
  • identified gaps in existing infrastructure;
  • technical questions for any later phase.

End-of-phase gate

GO / MODIFY / NO-GO

GO: evidence supports a bounded next-stage pilot or resource-recovery unit.

MODIFY: a different intervention or more evidence is required.

NO-GO: additional infrastructure is unnecessary, duplicative, unsafe, uneconomic or unsupported.

Long-term vision

Replication is an outcome to earn.

YSI's long-term objective is not to accumulate facilities. It is to establish whether a localized operating model can reduce environmental leakage, produce useful recovery evidence and work inside Freetown's existing waste architecture.

If a later pilot proves repeatable, compliant and economically defensible, replicated units could be considered. If the evidence says the city is better served by strengthening existing infrastructure instead, that is also a valid result.

Project status

Phase 0 development. Evidence and operating relationships next.

No site, equipment, permits, full capital budget or proven technical performance is being claimed. The current task is to constrain the design through authority/provider realities, measurement methodology, candidate sites and a costed Phase 0 budget.
