YSI Africa flagship development concept

Local Waste.
Local Value.
Local Energy.

YSI Africa is developing a modular circular infrastructure model for Freetown that intercepts waste closer to where it is produced, measures it, recovers useful materials, and uses suitable residual material to support the system's own process-energy needs.

LocalizedMeasuredRecoverableReplicable
THE CORE IDEA Capture earlier. Recover more. Build value before disposal.

One pilot catchment. One Circular Resource Unit. Then a better second unit, a standardized third, and a connected Freetown Circular Grid.

What the system does

Move less waste. Recover more value.

Strategically placed skip bins intercept waste within a defined pilot catchment. A YSI-controlled collection system moves that waste to a Circular Resource Unit for weighing, inspection, sorting, separation and routing.

Recyclables move toward verified value pathways. Organics move toward appropriate biological treatment. Special waste is controlled. True residual is disposed of safely. Suitable combustible residual material moves into the RDF pathway.

The 16-step operating loop

From interception to a citywide network.

The illustrations will be added later. For now, the process is shown as a simple operating sequence.

01

Intercept

Covered skip bins capture waste close to where it is produced.

02

Collect

YSI-controlled haulage services bins according to actual use.

03

Measure

Every load is weighed, recorded and linked to its collection point.

04

Inspect

Incoming waste is checked for hazards, contamination and unsuitable items.

05

Sort

Materials move through the sorting process for identification.

06

Separate

Recyclables, organics, energy candidates, special waste and true residual are divided.

07

Route

Each separated stream moves to its designated recovery or control pathway.

08

Prepare Energy Stream

Suitable combustible residual is cleaned and prepared for RDF processing.

09

Dry

Excess moisture is removed from the energy-candidate material.

10

Shred

Dried material is reduced to the required size.

11

Produce RDF

The prepared material becomes refuse-derived fuel.

12

Heat the Dryer

RDF is used in a controlled thermal system and recovered heat returns to Step 09.

13

Capture Residues

Bottom ash, fly ash and pollution-control residues are kept separate.

14

Recover or Control Ash

Tested bottom ash may support construction use; other residues are controlled.

15

Replicate the Unit

Unit 01 informs a better Unit 02 and a standardized Unit 03.

16

Connect the Network

Multiple Circular Resource Units form the Freetown Circular Grid.

The process-energy loop

RDF helps dry the next batch.

Suitable non-recyclable combustible material is prepared into RDF. That RDF is then used in a controlled thermal system to generate useful heat.

The recovered heat feeds back into the drying step for the next batch of energy-candidate material. The central technical question is whether the loop can substantially support its own drying demand without significant outside fuel.

ENERGY CANDIDATE → DRY → SHRED → RDF → CONTROLLED HEAT → BACK TO DRYER

Pilot 1

Measure first. Prove what works. Build the standard.

YSI Circular Systems Lab — Unit 01 is the evidence-building stage.

Evidence

Real waste, real measurements

Pilot 1 establishes the actual waste composition, recovery potential, RDF performance, energy balance, environmental requirements and economics needed to engineer the mature system.

Economics

Full-system cost

YSI will measure collection, processing, maintenance, shared infrastructure and final disposal together rather than treating unit cost as system cost.

Engineering

Technology follows evidence

Exact machinery, operating sequence and thermal design will be selected through characterization, engineering, safety review and regulatory approval.

Material intelligence

Every bin becomes a data point.

YSI will track where waste originates, how quickly bins fill, what each catchment produces, what can be recovered, what creates value and what still requires disposal.

That operating evidence improves every new unit.

The vision

From one catchment to the Freetown Circular Grid.

Unit 01 tests the operating concept. Unit 02 improves and replicates it. Unit 03 establishes the standard. Multiple units connect into a citywide network.

Once the system is proven, YSI can expand the model throughout Sierra Leone and into other cities and countries facing similar waste challenges.

Project status

Development stage. Architecture defined. Evidence next.

The system is a development concept, not a claim of proven economics or engineering performance. Pilot 1 will determine the equipment, energy balance, emissions controls, safe ash pathways, operating costs and regulatory requirements.
