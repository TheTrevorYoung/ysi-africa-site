YSI Africa flagship development concept

Local Waste.
Local Value.
Better Systems.

YSI Africa is developing a localized waste-interception and circular resource-recovery pilot for Freetown that would intercept waste closer to where it leaks into the environment, weigh and characterize it, sort the material streams, test realistic recovery pathways, and document what works before replication.

LocalizedMeasuredEvidence-ledReplicable
THE CORE IDEA Capture earlier. Measure first. Recover what is realistic.

One defined pilot catchment. One evidence-building resource-recovery unit. Then improve, standardize and replicate only what the data supports.

What the pilot would test

Intercept waste. Understand it. Test better pathways.

Strategically placed covered skip bins would intercept waste within a defined pilot catchment. Waste would then move through a compliant collection and haulage arrangement to a resource-recovery unit for weighing, inspection, characterization, sorting, separation and routing.

Recyclables would move only toward verified recovery pathways. Organics and other material streams would be assessed for appropriate treatment. Special waste would be controlled. True residual would be handled responsibly. Alternative-fuel pathways would be evaluated only if characterization and technical testing show they are justified.

The 16-step proposed operating loop

From interception to evidence-led replication.

This sequence describes a proposed pilot design. It does not represent currently operating YSI infrastructure or technically validated production.

01

Intercept

Covered skip bins would capture waste close to where it is produced or leaks into the environment.

02

Collect

A compliant collection and haulage arrangement would service bins according to actual use.

03

Measure

Each load would be weighed, recorded and linked to its collection point.

04

Inspect

Incoming waste would be checked for hazards, contamination and unsuitable items.

05

Characterize & Sort

Waste would be characterized and sorted so the actual material mix can be understood.

06

Separate

Recoverable materials, organics, special waste, possible energy candidates and true residual would be separated.

07

Route

Each stream would move only toward an appropriate verified recovery, treatment or disposal pathway.

08

Assess Residual Options

Suitable non-recyclable combustible material may be identified as a candidate for alternative-fuel testing.

09

Test Moisture & Drying

Moisture and drying requirements would be measured before any fuel-preparation pathway is selected.

10

Prepare Test Material

If justified, candidate material would be prepared to the specifications required for controlled technical testing.

11

Test RDF/SRF Feasibility

Alternative-fuel potential would be evaluated through composition, calorific, contamination, environmental and economic evidence.

12

Evaluate Process Heat

Only if technically justified would YSI evaluate whether a controlled thermal pathway could support useful process heat.

13

Capture Test Residues

Any controlled thermal testing would require separate capture and analysis of bottom ash, fly ash and pollution-control residues.

14

Assess Residue Pathways

No ash or residue would be assigned a reuse pathway without appropriate testing and regulatory review.

15

Replicate the Unit

Unit 01 would inform a better Unit 02 and, only after evidence, a more standardized operating model.

16

Connect the Network

Replication into a broader network would occur only if the pilot demonstrates practical, compliant and financially credible results.

A downstream technical hypothesis

Could suitable residual material support process energy?

One pathway YSI may investigate is whether a portion of non-recyclable combustible residual material can meet the technical, environmental and economic requirements for RDF or SRF.

No such pathway is assumed. Waste characterization must come first, followed by testing of moisture, calorific value, chlorine and contamination, emissions and residue controls, equipment needs, economics, regulatory requirements and potential users.

CHARACTERIZE → TEST → VALIDATE → ONLY THEN CONSIDER RDF/SRF OR PROCESS HEAT

Pilot 1

Measure first. Prove what works. Build the standard.

The first unit is an evidence-building stage, not a claim of proven industrial performance.

Evidence

Real waste, real measurements

Pilot 1 would establish actual waste composition, recovery potential, operating requirements and unit costs, and determine whether any downstream RDF/SRF pathway is technically, environmentally and economically justified.

Economics

Full-system cost

YSI intends to measure collection, processing, maintenance, shared infrastructure and final disposal together rather than treating one unit operation as the cost of the whole system.

Engineering

Technology follows evidence

Exact machinery, operating sequence and any thermal design would be selected only after characterization, engineering, safety review and regulatory assessment.

Material intelligence

Every collection point becomes a data point.

YSI intends to track where waste originates, how quickly bins fill, what each catchment produces, what can realistically be recovered, what creates value and what still requires responsible disposal.

That operating evidence would inform every later design decision.

The vision

Prove one localized system before replication.

Unit 01 would test the operating concept. A second unit should only improve and replicate elements supported by the evidence. Standardization and any wider network would come later.

If the model proves practical, compliant and economically credible, YSI can assess replication elsewhere in Freetown, Sierra Leone and potentially other cities facing similar waste challenges.

Project status

Development stage. System concept defined. Evidence and engineering next.

The pilot is a development concept, not a claim of proven economics, infrastructure or engineering performance. Characterization and field measurement must determine the equipment, viable recovery pathways, environmental controls, operating costs, regulatory requirements and any justified downstream energy pathway.
