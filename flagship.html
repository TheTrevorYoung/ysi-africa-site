YSI Africa flagship development concept

Local Waste.
Local Value.
Better Systems.

YSI Africa is developing a localized waste-interception and circular resource-recovery pilot for Freetown. The model would intercept waste within a defined catchment, weigh and characterize it, sort it into practical material streams, test recovery pathways, and document the evidence needed before replication.

LocalizedMeasuredEvidence-ledReplicable
THE CORE IDEA Capture earlier. Measure first. Recover what is realistic.

One defined pilot catchment. One Circular Resource Unit. Then use verified operating evidence to decide what should be improved, replicated or stopped.

What the pilot would test

A localized system built around evidence.

Proposed covered skip bins would intercept waste within a defined pilot catchment. Compliant collection and haulage would move that waste to a Circular Resource Unit for weighing, inspection, characterization, sorting, separation and routing.

Recyclables, organics, special waste, true residual and potential energy-candidate material would follow different pathways only where technical, environmental and economic evidence supports them.

The 16-step operating loop

From interception to a citywide network.

Intercept

Proposed covered skip bins capture waste close to where leakage occurs within a defined pilot area.

02

Collect

Compliant collection and haulage move pilot waste to the Circular Resource Unit.

03

Measure

Every incoming load is weighed, recorded and linked to its collection point.

04

Inspect

Incoming waste is checked for hazards, contamination and unsuitable items.

05

Characterize & Sort

Pilot waste is characterized and sorted to establish what the catchment actually produces.

06

Separate

Materials are divided into practical categories such as recyclables, organics, potential energy candidates, special waste and true residual.

07

Route

Each stream moves only to a validated recovery, treatment or disposal pathway.

08

Assess Residual Options

Suitable non-recyclable material may be tested as potential RDF/SRF feedstock where justified.

09

Test Moisture & Drying

Energy-candidate material is assessed for moisture and drying requirements before any fuel specification is assumed.

10

Prepare Test Material

Test material may be size-reduced or otherwise prepared to evaluate handling and fuel characteristics.

11

Test RDF/SRF Feasibility

Prepared material is tested against relevant fuel-quality, safety and offtake requirements.

12

Evaluate Process Heat

If justified, controlled thermal trials can test whether recovered heat can offset part of the process-energy demand.

13

Capture Test Residues

Any thermal testing must separately capture bottom ash, fly ash and pollution-control residues.

14

Assess Residue Pathways

No ash reuse is assumed; tested residues require characterization and compliant reuse or disposal pathways.

15

Replicate the Unit

Evidence from Unit 01 would determine whether a second and later standardized unit should be developed.

16

Connect the Network

Only proven, compliant units would be connected into a wider Freetown Circular Grid.

A technical hypothesis to test

Could suitable residual material support process energy?

Some non-recyclable combustible material may have value as RDF/SRF feedstock, but no fuel or thermal pathway is assumed. Controlled testing would be required to determine whether a safe, compliant and useful fuel can actually be produced from the pilot waste stream.

If justified, heat recovery could be tested to see whether it can offset part of the drying-energy demand. The central questions are fuel quality, emissions control, safe ash handling, energy balance, economics and regulatory approval.

Pilot 1

Measure first. Prove what works. Build the standard.

YSI Circular Systems Lab — Unit 01 is the evidence-building stage.

Evidence

Real waste, real measurements

Pilot 1 establishes the actual waste composition, recovery potential, fuel-feedstock characteristics, energy-trial results, environmental requirements and economics needed to determine what a mature system should include.

Economics

Full-system cost

YSI will measure collection, processing, maintenance, shared infrastructure and final disposal together rather than treating unit cost as system cost.

Engineering

Technology follows evidence

Exact machinery, operating sequence and any thermal design would be selected through characterization, engineering, safety review, regulatory requirements and validated offtake pathways.

Material intelligence

Every bin becomes a data point.

YSI will track where waste originates, how quickly bins fill, what each catchment produces, what can realistically be recovered, what may create value and what still requires disposal.

That operating evidence improves every next design decision.

The vision

Prove one localized system before replication.

Unit 01 tests the operating concept. Only after operational, environmental, technical and economic validation would YSI consider Unit 02 and a later standardized Unit 03.

If the model proves repeatable, multiple approved units could form a wider Freetown network and eventually inform adaptation elsewhere in Sierra Leone.

Project status

Development stage. Architecture defined. Evidence next.

The system is a development concept, not a claim of proven infrastructure, economics or engineering performance. Pilot 1 will determine the equipment, energy balance, emissions controls, safe residue pathways, operating costs and regulatory requirements.
